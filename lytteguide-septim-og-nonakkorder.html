Dominant & Major 7ths & 9ths

Ten chords across four categories (dominant and major, sevenths and ninths), each with real staff notation and a recording where the chord is doing real, audible work. Every song links to a Spotify search.

Pick a group with the tabs below. Each card has the chord in notation, a play button, and songs where you can clearly hear the chord. Press play to hear it.

C7

1 – 3 – 5 – ♭7, the blues workhorse
C – E – G – B♭
  • Sweet Home Chicago
    Robert Johnson. The whole tune rides on the I–IV–V7 blues turnaround; you can hear the dominant 7th resolve every time the band lands back on the "one" chord at the end of each verse line
    Spotify

E7(♯9)

1 – 3 – 5 – ♭7 – ♯9, the Hendrix chord
E – G♯ – B – D – F double sharp (the ♯9 rubs against the major third)
  • Purple Haze
    Jimi Hendrix, 1967. The song opens with a tense riff on B♭ and E. Then the E7♯9 chord comes in, hit hard on guitar, and it keeps coming back under the verse
    Spotify
  • Foxy Lady
    Jimi Hendrix, 1967. The song fades in on guitar feedback. Then the main riff comes in, built around F♯7♯9, the same chord moved up a whole step
    Spotify

G7

  • Ballade No. 1 in G minor, Op. 23
    Chopin. Listen through the piece's big transitional passages: the harmony keeps pulling toward new keys through dominant 7ths like this one before each new theme resolves

E9

A9

D9

Fmaj7

1 – 3 – 5 – 7
F – A – C – E
  • The Girl from Ipanema
    Antônio Carlos Jobim. The tune opens on this exact chord (in the standard's home key of F) and keeps returning to it every time the melody comes back to "tall and tan and young and lovely"
    Spotify

Gmaj9

1 – 3 – 5 – 7 – 9
G – B – D – F♯ – A
  • Peg
    Steely Dan, 1977. The keyboard chords in the intro and verses are full of major 7th and major 9th colors, the lush sound Steely Dan is known for
    Spotify

D♭maj9

1 – 3 – 5 – 7 – 9
D♭ – F – A♭ – C – E♭. In the piece it is an added-9th color over D♭ in the bass, not a strict jazz stack
  • Clair de Lune
    Debussy. Listen to the arpeggiated right-hand figures in the middle section: the melody note sitting a 9th above the bass, left ringing rather than resolved, is the signature impressionist "added 9th" haze this piece is known for
    Spotify

E♭maj9

1 – 3 – 5 – 7 – 9
E♭ – G – B♭ – D – F. 9th-like color from suspensions, not a strict jazz stack
  • Nocturne in E♭ major, Op. 9 No. 2
    Chopin. Near the end of each phrase, the melody leans on an ornamental turn that hangs above the harmony as an unresolved 9th before settling back down. That lingering, singing dissonance is the Romantic-era ancestor of the jazz maj9 sound
    Spotify
